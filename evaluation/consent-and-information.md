# Participant information and consent

Read or send this before the session. The participant keeps a copy.

**This template still needs checking against your institution's ethics requirements
before use.** It follows the usual shape of a minimal-risk usability study, but the
institution's rules on recording, retention and consent wording govern, not this file. If
your department requires a specific form or a recorded verbal consent, use theirs.

---

## What this study is

I am a student on COMM7660. I have built a small web tool for my final project and I am
testing whether it is understandable and usable. This is coursework, not commercial
research.

## What you will do

You will use the tool for about 15 minutes while I watch and take notes. I will ask you to
do a couple of tasks and then a few questions about your impression.

## What I record

| Recorded | Not recorded |
|---|---|
| What you do in the tool (which controls you use, whether a result appeared) | Your name |
| Errors or confusion you hit, in my words | Your contact details |
| Short quotes of what you say, attributed to a code such as "P3" | Anything identifying you |
| Rough timing | Screen or audio recording, unless you separately agree to it |

If I want to record audio or video, I will ask you at the time and you can decline without
affecting anything else.

## Risks

None beyond ordinary use of a web page. The tool is not a commercial product and has no
real users' data in it. All the games it lists are public Steam titles.

## Your choices

- You can stop at any point, for any reason, with no explanation.
- You can decline any question.
- You can ask me to delete anything you said, up to the point where I submit the work.
- You do not have to be positive about it. Criticism is far more useful to me than praise,
  and being blunt will not offend me.

## How the data is used

Notes go into a coursework report and a private repository that my instructor can access.
Quotes appear without your name. The data is not published elsewhere and not shared with
anyone beyond the course.

## What I ask of you

One thing: **do not look at the tool before the session.** The first time you see it is
the thing I am measuring. If you have already seen it, tell me and we will skip the
session rather than produce misleading notes.
